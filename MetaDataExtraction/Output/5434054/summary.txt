Implementation and results: The proposed VCO was implemented in a 65nm CMOS process. Figure 2.7.4 shows the circuit together with its output differential buffer, which creates a 2ω0 disturbance on the supply rail. Note that the 1/f noise from the buffer transistors is upconverted as supply noise around 2ω0 then further down-converted at ω0 by the VCO switching as 1/f amplitude noise across the tank, finally giving rise to 1/f3 phase noise [4]. Since 1/f3 noise in the VCO core is very low, the network RF-CF-RF is needed to filter out the signal at 2ω0, avoiding any residual 1/f3 contribution. The VCO tank features a quality factor of about 10 at 3GHz. The capacitance is made of two banks of 20 switched metal-metal capacitors and two thick-oxide MOS varactors. All the resistors are poly type. The widths of the pand nFETs are in a ratio of two to one, in order to bias the oscillation voltage at the middle of the supply rails, and so are their drain parasitic capacitances. As a result, the drain resistors have been scaled accordingly. (The R and Cp values reported in Fig. 2.7.3 refer to the nFETs). The measured tuning range spans between 3.0 and 3.6GHz with a maximum power consumption of 0.7mW from the 1.2V supply (excluding buffers). A VCO with identical topology but with no drain resistors was fabricated as a reference on the same die (Figure 2.7.7 shows the photo of both oscillators). Figure 2.7.5 shows the phase noise spectra of the two free-running oscillators. The proposed circuit outperforms the standard topology by 10dB at 1kHz. As expected, the 1/f2 noise of about -114dBc/Hz at 1MHz is not degraded by resistors, leading to a Figure of Merit of about 187dB. Figure 2.7.6 shows that the flicker noise suppression is obtained over the whole 3.0-to-3.6GHz tuning range, confirming the wideband nature of the proposed technique. In conclusion, a wideband technique suppressing 1/f noise upconversion has been successfully demonstrated in a 65nm CMOS VCO. The solution relies on insertion of resistors in series with the drains of the core FETs. The non-resonant solution allows upconversion suppression over a wide frequency range, while the resistor placements avoid degradation of both the oscillator start-up and “white” 1/f2 phase noise as well.
