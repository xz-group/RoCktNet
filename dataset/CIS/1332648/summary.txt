# A Fully Integrated 0.13µm CMOS 10Gb Ethernet Transceiver with XAUI Interface

VENUE
"2004"

FIGURE NUMBER
"Figure 9.3.1 shows the overall block diagram of the 10Gb Ethernet transceiver. The chip consists of a 10.3125Gb/s Serializer/Deserializer (SerDes), a quad 3.125Gb/s SerDes, and a physical coding sublayer (PCS) block in-between. The 10Gb/s serializer transmits the bit stream retimed at a 10GHz full-rate clock generated by an LC oscillator, in order to eliminate the deterministic jitter caused by the non-uniform phase spacings, which presents in most time-multiplexing transmitters based on multiphase clocks. The 10Gb/s deserializer recovers the clock and data from the serial data stream, using binary phase detection with a dead-zone [2]. The use of multiphase clocks in this 10Gb/s CDR favored a ring-type VCO for small area. The quad 3.125Gb/s interface, on the other hand, employs blind oversampling CDR technique [3]. Since a blind oversampling CDR simply selects the recovered data among the equally-spaced samples spanning a bit period instead of continuously adjusting the sample timing, it is possible that the four CDRs share a common multiphase clock domain and synchronization is readily achieved. Also, since the only analog block is the multiphase clock generator being shared, the rest of the CDR circuits can be synthesized along with the PCS block, offering good portability."
